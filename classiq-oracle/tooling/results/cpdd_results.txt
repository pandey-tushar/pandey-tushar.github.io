CP-DD: disjoint-ancilla (q12-14 / q15-17) + disjoint-data-write R1/R2t pair
============================================================================
Hard cap 120 min. Files: cpdd_r1fix.py (R1 with the x3 collision fixed),
cpdd_pair.py (pairing measurements). No files deleted/modified outside the
cpdd_ prefix.

STAGE 1: ancilla count under the 3+3 (then 4+2) split
--------------------------------------------------
Method: emit.rect.emit_rect (the project's own from-scratch form-search +
backtracking host-assignment emitter, NOT cpal's hand forms) run at k=3,4,5
ancillas for both rects, same-register hosting only (x-axis hosts confined
to x0-5+its own scratch, y-axis to y0-5+its own scratch -- the emitter's
native mode). This is a DIFFERENT method from CP-DC's hand uncompute-lemma
analysis, so it is an independent check of CP-DC's "floor = 5" finding.

  R1  rect(2,26,29,53):  k=3 FAIL (no wire-feasible emission, incl.
                                   escalation tier)
                          k=4 FAIL (same, 95.6s)
                          k=5 PASS depth=57 cx=75  (19.0s)
  R2t rect(27,48,39,43): k=3 FAIL (46.7s)
                          k=4 FAIL (93.4s)
                          k=5 PASS depth=67 cx=85  (68.1s)

RESULT: independently confirms CP-DC's hand-derived floor of 5 ancillas
per block -- via a completely different, automated, from-scratch form
search, not just cpal's specific hand forms. Both R1 and R2t need >=5
ancillas; 5+5=10 > 6 available, so no split of the fixed 6-wire ancilla
pool (q12-17) into disjoint per-block ranges fits BOTH blocks, at ANY
split point: 3+3 fails (this task's target), 4+2 fails (R1 needs 5 not
4; R2t needs 5 not 2), and even 4+4 or 5+5 exceed the 6-wire budget.
THE HARD ANCILLA-RANGE REQUIREMENT (R1 on q12-14 only, R2t on q15-17
only) IS NOT REACHABLE within this budget, by either method tried
(CP-DC's hand replay-uncompute lemma, or this stage's automated
from-scratch search). No further ancilla-reduction attempt was made
(a structurally different boolean form family, of the kind CP-BP..CP-CJ
spent many hours deriving for the much harder D2 disk, would be needed;
out of scope for the remaining time after this finding).

STAGE 1b: the data-write collision (separate from the ancilla question)
--------------------------------------------------
Per the task's correction to CP-DC ("previous pass kept x3 hosted by
both blocks -- do not repeat"): computed the exact write-set (data wires
0-11 ever XOR-accumulated by a CX/CCX/RC3X TARGET -- transient X-gate
negative-control polarity flips, which bracket and cancel around a
single gate, are NOT counted as hosting) for the shipped hand forms:

  cpal_r1  (unmodified): {x2, x3, y3, y5}
  cpal_r2t (unmodified): {x0, x1, x3, x5, y2}
  Intersection: {x3}  <-- the one real collision (R1 hosts e=x3^x4 on
                          x3; R2t hosts G on x3).

FIX (cpdd_r1fix.py): e is symmetric in x3,x4 (e = x3^x4), so host it on
x4 instead. This needs 2 CX instead of 1 on the J-fold (originally
`x2 ^= x4_raw` directly; now x4 holds e, so `x2 ^= x4(=e); x2 ^= x3(raw)`
recovers x2 ^= x4_raw since e^x3 = x4_raw). Everything else in axis_x is
unchanged; axis_y is unchanged.

  cpdd_r1fix write-set: {x2, x4, y3, y5}
  cpal_r2t   write-set: {x0, x1, x3, x5, y2}
  Intersection: EMPTY.  Disjoint data writes ACHIEVED.

Verified: classical replay 0/4096 mismatches (both x and y predicates).
Solo block: opt2=opt3 depth=51 cx=73 (was cpal_r1 43/73 -- the fix costs
+8 depth, +0 cx, solo). Statevector raw err=2.44e-15 leak=3.2e-32 PASS;
opt2 err=3.71e-15 leak=1.6e-30 PASS.

Ancilla wires used by cpdd_r1fix: UNCHANGED from cpal_r1 -- still all of
q12-17 (AA=12,AB=13,SX=[14,15],SY=[16,17]). The x3 fix is a pure
data-write fix; it does not by itself reduce ancilla count (stage 1
already showed that reduction is not reachable in budget).

STAGE 2: pairing
--------------------------------------------------
Measured (cpdd_pair.py, opt2 = opt3, u3/cx basis; assert intersection
empty is satisfied by construction per stage 1b):

  A. serial concatenation, R1fix + R2t, shared ancillas 12-17 (as
     shipped -- the ancilla-range split from stage 1 not being
     reachable, both blocks use the full 12-17 pool, non-concurrently):
     depth=88 cx=144.  Strict elementwise statevector check (built from
     |+>^12|0>^6, compared to (-1)^(f1+f2)/64 term by term, no phase
     division): err=1.04e-16, leak=3.03e-17, PASS.
     (was shipped cpal_r1+cpal_r2t serial: depth 85/142 per CP-DC/CP-AL;
     the x3 fix costs +3 depth, +2 cx here after transpile overlap,
     less than its solo +8/+0 cost.)

  B. ILLEGAL-WIDTH ceiling probe (per the project's established
     delete/ceiling-probe convention, e.g. CP-CD/CE/CG/CP-DC): R2t's
     ancillas remapped to a disjoint lane 18-23 (width 24), data wires
     0-11 unchanged (still the disjoint write-sets from stage 1b),
     executed serially (no interleaving yet):
     depth=88 cx=144 -- IDENTICAL to A.

  C. round-robin GATE INTERLEAVE at the same illegal disjoint-ancilla
     width 24 (R1's and R2t's ops merged one-for-one in emission order):
     depth=90 cx=149 -- WORSE than plain serial (A/B), even with
     (i) fully disjoint data writes (stage 1b fix) AND (ii) fully
     disjoint ancillas (illegal unlimited-width ceiling, no budget
     constraint at all).

  D. round-robin interleave at the REAL 18-qubit width with ancillas
     SHARED (12-17, since the disjoint ancilla-range split itself was
     not reachable): depth=115 cx=146, strict statevector err=3.1e-2,
     leak=1.6e-2 -- FAILS (as expected: R1's and R2t's accumulators
     AA=12/AB=13 are the SAME physical qubits, so interleaving two
     blocks that both accumulate phase onto q12/q13 corrupts both --
     this is not a valid circuit at all, only serial composition is
     legal when ancillas are shared; included to make the point
     explicit, not as a candidate result).

DECISIVE FINDING: fixing the ONE real data-write collision (x3) and
even granting UNLIMITED disjoint ancilla width (case C, the strongest
possible ceiling) still does not beat plain serial concatenation --
depth goes UP (85->90 relative to the original shipped forms, or
88->90 for the collision-fixed forms). This independently reconfirms
CP-DC's stage-2 conclusion under the corrected (disjoint-write) forms:
the binding constraint on pairing R1/R2t is each block's OWN internal
dependency chain length (R1fix's critical path is 51 layers solo, R2t's
is 39), not cross-block resource contention on data wires or ancillas.
Interleaving two SEPARATE, SELF-CONTAINED C;CZ;C^-1 blocks cannot beat
their sum-of-critical-paths-with-transpiler-overlap unless the gates
THEMSELVES are restructured to share dependency chains (the CP-BG/CP-CB
"joint forward" style union, which needs a genuinely merged circuit
design, not a scheduling trick on two separately-derived blocks).

Gate "pair < 80" is NOT met (best is 88, case A/B; interleaving strictly
loses). No cpdd_oracle.qasm attempt was made: stage 3 was conditioned
on stage 2 achieving pair < 85, which it did not (88 > 85, and 90 for
interleaved variants), so per the task's own stage-3 gate this was
correctly not pursued further within the time cap.

CONCLUSION
--------------------------------------------------
Two separable questions were asked; both come back negative, but for
DIFFERENT reasons, now cleanly separated (which CP-DC did not fully
separate, since it never fixed the x3 collision):

  1. ANCILLA-RANGE disjointness (R1 on q12-14, R2t on q15-17): NOT
     REACHABLE.  Independently reconfirmed via a from-scratch automated
     form search (emit.rect, not just cpal's hand forms) that BOTH
     blocks need >=5 ancillas; 5+5 > 6 available, so no split (3+3,
     4+2, or otherwise) fits within the fixed 6-wire ancilla pool.

  2. DATA-WRITE disjointness: ACHIEVED (the x3 collision was the only
     one; fixed by re-hosting e on x4 for +8 depth solo / +3 merged).
     But achieving it PROVES INSUFFICIENT ON ITS OWN: even with
     disjoint writes AND an illegal unlimited-width disjoint-ancilla
     ceiling, interleaving R1fix and R2t's gates is WORSE than serial
     (90 vs 88), because the two blocks' critical paths are each
     already close to their own per-block floor (CP-CF/CP-AH-style
     AND-depth arguments) and share no computation to overlap --
     disjointness makes concurrent SCHEDULING legal, but there is
     nothing productive to schedule concurrently, since neither
     block's critical path shortens by having a scheduling slot free
     next to it if the OTHER block's gates on THAT SAME PHYSICAL WIRE
     don't exist (which is exactly what disjoint writes guarantees --
     they never touch, so there is no shared resource to parallelize
     across two 18-wire-wide circuits that are each already using most
     of the 18 wires for their OWN critical path).

Both gates (ancilla split, pair<80) are NOT met. This matches and
sharpens CP-DC's conclusion with the confound (x3 collision) removed:
the result is not an artifact of that collision.
