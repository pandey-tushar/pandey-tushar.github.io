CP-ED: makespan-scheduled re-synthesis of the logo phase oracle
===============================================================
Every number below is MEASURED with qiskit 2.4,
transpile(basis_gates=['u3','cx'], optimization_level=2 and 3), all-to-all.
opt2 == opt3 in every row that was measured at both levels.
Exactness everywhere = cpae_core.sim_exact: all 4096 data inputs replayed,
phase compared to (-1)^F and the 18-wire permutation checked back to identity.
Lines that say "not measured" were not measured.

STEP 0 -- PRIMITIVE AND CHAINING CALIBRATION (cped_cal.py, opt2 = opt3)
  RCCX 7 / 3      RC3X 13 / 6     CZ 3 / 1     CCZ 10 / 6
  C3Z (3 controls) 27 / 14        C4Z (4 controls) 65 / 36
  k fully disjoint RCCX, k = 1..6 : depth 7 (cx 3k)  -- six fit in one layer
  same target, k RCCX   :  7 12 17 22 27      -> +5 per extra write
  same target, k RC3X   : 13 21 29 37 45      -> +8 per extra write
  fan-out as control slot a, k RCCX :  7  8  9 10 11   -> +1 per extra gate
  fan-out as control slot b, k RCCX :  7 12 17 22 27   -> +5 per extra gate
  ladder target -> slot a, k levels : 11 15 19 23 27   -> +4 per level
  ladder target -> slot b, k levels : 13 19 25         -> +6 per level
  fan-out RC3X slot a   : 13 18 23 28         -> +5 per extra gate
  k CZ sharing a wire   :  3  5  7  9 11 13   -> +2 per extra CZ
  CX feeding slot a or slot b then RCCX : 7 (free).  CX into the target : 8
  mirror block C ; CZ ; C^-1 with forward 7 : 15 = 2*forward + 1
  NEW FACT (this session): slot a fan-out is +1 and slot b fan-out is +5.
  Earlier sessions recorded slot a as +4; the measurement above says +1, so a
  value that controls many Margolus gates is nearly free if it is fed into
  slot a.  Putting the raw data wires into slot a is worth real layers (see
  STEP 4).
  RCCX control symmetry: RCCX(a,b,t) and RCCX(b,a,t) are DIFFERENT unitaries
  (relative phase) but the SAME permutation, so swapping the two controls of
  a Margolus and of its mirror partner leaves a mirror block exact.
  RC3X is symmetric in its first two controls as a unitary.

STEP 4 -- A NEW, EXACT, LOW-AND-DEPTH FACTORISATION OF D2 (cped_d2*.py)
  Verified by truth table and by 4096-input replay (0 mismatches, all
  registers restored):
    D2 = XOR_{k=1..5} Rg_k(x) N_k(y)        (rank 5, staircase)
    x side, P = x5 ~x4, lo3 = x & 7 :
      I_j = P * ( x3 [lo3 <= r_j]  ^  ~x3 [lo3 >= 8-r_j] ),  r = 8,7,6,4,2
      plus the single point x = 48 ; Rg_k = I_{6-k} ^ I_{7-k}
    y side, C = ~y5 ~y4, D = ~y5 y4, lo4 = y & 15 :
      N_k = D [lo4 <= 3+t_k] ^ C [lo4 >= 19-t_k],  t = 8,7,6,4,2
      (k = 5 additionally needs lo4 >= 1)
  AND-depth 3, against AND-depth 8 for the shipped D2.
  MEASURED (cpdh_core.measure; every row replayed 0 mismatches, 0 dirty):
    carriers            wires  scratch  fwd opt2  block opt2   block opt3
    1 per side            43      31      107      274 / 306   274 / 306
    2 per side            49      37       84      224 / 310   224 / 310
    all 10 atoms resident 51      39       73      149 / 298   149 / 298
  Shipped D2 at 18 wires, for comparison: block 125 / 239.
  So the AND-depth-3 form loses to the shipped AND-depth-8 block even when it
  is given 39 scratch registers.
  Slot-a substitution on the same netlist (raw data wires moved from RCCX
  slot b to slot a, nothing else changed): forward 81 -> 73, block 165 -> 149.
  MECHANISM MEASURED: a rank-r shape fired as a staircase needs 2r-1 = 9
  alternating carrier updates (x, y, x, y, ...), each update 1 to 3 RCCX
  writes on one wire at +5 layers each, and the CZ between them orders the two
  sides.  That is the 107 at one carrier per side; two carriers per side hide
  half of it (84); ten registers hide all of it (73).

  demand.  Every emitted schedule is replayed over all 4096 inputs.
  Same verified D2 DAG as STEP 4, K = ancillas, wires = 12 + K :
    K    wires   block depth / cx (opt2 = opt3)   replay
     6     18     infeasible for this DAG under this allocator
     7     19     infeasible
     8     20     infeasible
    10     22     infeasible
    12     24     infeasible
    14     26     551 / 524      err 0.0e+00  mism 0
    16     28     403 / 368      err 0.0e+00  mism 0

  The curve is the pebbler's, not a lower bound: the greedy schedule at 26
  registers (294) is worse than the static schedule at 39 (149), so the
  allocator, not the DAG, sets these numbers.

  CALIBRATION OF THE PEBBLER ITSELF (cped_rect.py) -- how far it is from a
  hand-tuned block on the two EASY rank-1 shapes, all rows replayed exactly
  (err 0.0e+00, 0 of 4096 mismatches):
    shape    K    wires   block depth / cx (opt2 = opt3)   shipped block
    R2t      6      18          187 / 189                       39
    R1       6,7,8             infeasible                       43
    R1       9      21          177 / 181                       43
    R1      10      22          137 / 175                       43
    R1      12      24          161 / 163                       43
    R1^R2t  6..12               infeasible                      82 (39+43)
  The pebbler is 3 to 5 times worse than the shipped hand-tuned block on a
  single rectangle, and it cannot schedule two rectangles at 12 registers
  although it schedules each one separately at 6 and 10.  So the STEP 5
  feasibility thresholds are ALLOCATOR artefacts, not properties of the
  problem, and every makespan in STEP 5 is an upper bound for this pipeline
  only.  The honest statement the table supports is the RATIO: on this
  pipeline, halving the register budget from 26 to 14 costs a factor 1.9 in
  makespan (294 -> 551), and below 14 the schedule does not exist at all.

STEP 6 -- CLEAN DATA-WIRE HOSTS (derived from the shapes, not measured as
  circuits this session)
  A value V hosted on data wire d leaves the wire holding d ^ V.  It is exact
  as a CZ operand when the partner operand W satisfies d * W = 0, and exact as
  an RCCX control when the OTHER control W satisfies d * W = 0.  From the
  shape supports:
    D2  y-atoms lie in [11,27] (y5 = 0)  -> wire y5 can carry an x-side value
    D1, R2t y-atoms lie in [35,47] (y4 = 0) -> wire y4 can carry an x-side value
    R1  x-atom is [2,26] (x5 = 0)        -> wire x5 can carry a y-side value
    D2  four inner x-atoms lie in [33,47] (x4 = 0) -> wire x4 for a y-side value
  That is at most 2 extra clean registers at any one time, i.e. 8 usable
  registers against the 14 the D2 DAG alone needs.

BINDING CONSTRAINT (measured)
  1. For the SHIPPED blocks the wall is the twelve DATA wires, not the six
     ancillas.  The four blocks perform 279 writes whose target is a data wire
     (D2 alone writes all twelve) against 132 ancilla writes, so no two blocks
     can be overlapped in time: full ASAP interleaving is wrong on 4096 of
     4096 inputs at 24, 30 and 36 wires.  Extra ancillas buy 0 layers.
     This is why 270 is the floor of the interleaving route and why every
     search (720 ancilla permutations per block, 11743 anneal evaluations,
     commutation-DAG list scheduling in CP-DP, 56 control re-orientations and
     240 transpiler seeds here) returns 270.
  2. For a data-read-only replacement -- the only kind that COULD overlap --
     the register count bites, but this session could not separate "the
     machine has too few registers" from "this pebbler is too weak".  The
     exact AND-depth-3 D2 DAG needs at least 14 registers under this pebbler
     and 39 to reach its own best makespan of 149; and the same pebbler needs
     10 registers and 137 layers for R1, which the shipped code does in 6
     registers and 43 layers.  The second figure says the pebbler is the
     binding term in rows 5 and 6, not the width.
  3. Serialisation floor of the staircase: a rank-5 shape fires 2*5-1 = 9
     alternating carrier updates at +5 layers per RCCX write.  Measured on the
     exact D2 DAG: forward 107 with one carrier per side, 84 with two, 73 with
     all ten atoms resident.  The whole logo is LEFT (rank 5) plus D2 (rank 5),
     so two such staircases must run concurrently to reach 150; that needs
     4 carriers plus their live helpers, i.e. the 14-register figure above.
  THE NUMBER THAT PROVES CLAIM 1 (the one claim that is clean): the four
  shipped blocks perform 279 writes onto the twelve data wires, D2 writing all
  twelve, and a full ASAP interleave of them is wrong on 4096 of 4096 inputs
  at 24, 30 and 36 wires.  Overlap is therefore impossible at ANY ancilla
  count for this block set, which pins the route at the serial ledger, 270.
  Claims 2 and 3 are bounded by the synthesis and scheduling quality reached
  here, which STEP 5's calibration shows is 3 to 5 times off the shipped
  blocks; they do NOT bound the problem.

HONEST SCOPE
  The infeasibility numbers in STEP 5 are properties of ONE DAG under ONE
  greedy allocator; they are not a proof that no 6-ancilla AND-depth-3 D2
  exists.  The leaderboard entry at 142 / 557 cx / width 18 shows that some
  architecture does beat 150 at this width; its 557 cx against our 516 is
  about 186 RCCX-equivalents against 118, i.e. far more gate work spread over
  far more wire-layers than any block built here or in CP-DP/CP-EC uses.
  What those extra wire-layers carry was NOT MEASURED.
