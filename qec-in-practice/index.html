The repetition code

Step 1

Flip qubits in a row, watch the parity checks between neighbours fire, and decode them, which on a line is a majority vote. Then simulate the logical error rate against the physical error rate for d = 1, 3, 5 and 7 and set it beside the exact curve.

The course

What is this, and why does it matter?

Quantum computers are built from qubits, and qubits lose their information easily: small disturbances from their surroundings flip them, and nothing announces that it happened.

Error correction stores one reliable qubit in many unreliable ones. Checks keep comparing the qubits for disagreement without ever looking at the stored data, and a decoder uses the disagreements to find and undo errors.

This course lets you try that by hand, from a row of five qubits to the operations a future machine would run: gates between protected qubits, teleportation and lattice surgery.

The course

How the course works

The course is for readers who know what a qubit is and have met the H, T and CNOT gates; the checks, the decoders and the patches are introduced as they appear, and the glossary collects the terms. The figures run in your browser with nothing to install. The repetition code, decoding and threshold pages decode with a union-find decoder, and the threshold page also compares it with stored reference results from stim and pymatching. Lesson 1, the live run in lesson 2 and the stored results of lessons 1 to 4 correct with a lookup table of single errors instead. The live run in lesson 2 and the lattice surgery page simulate their circuits with a stabilizer simulator, and lessons 1 to 4 each end with a table of stored results from a noisy simulation in Qiskit Aer.

Four of the eight steps are the numbered lessons, Lessons 1 to 4, which share a step-through player and a results table; the other four are essays with their own figures. Each page has its text written out in full. Later steps build on earlier ones, so take them in order.

Tushar Pandey's own work in quantum error correction goes further, from an error-correction algorithm for the doubled semion model to surgery on qLDPC codes: see his research. For one family of qLDPC codes there is also the bivariate bicycle code explorer.
