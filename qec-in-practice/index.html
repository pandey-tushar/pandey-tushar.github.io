A course in eight interactive lessons

Quantum error correction in practice

How quantum error correction works, one lesson at a time: from a row of qubits guarded by parity checks to logical gates between surface code patches.

The course is for readers who know what a qubit is and have met the H, T and CNOT gates; the checks, the decoders and the patches are introduced as they appear. The figures run in your browser with nothing to install: a stabilizer simulator for the circuits and, from the decoding lesson on, a union-find decoder. Lessons 3 and 4 also show stored results from a noisy simulation. Each lesson is one page that you step through or run, with its text written out in full. Later lessons build on earlier ones, so take them in order.

Tushar Pandey's own work in quantum error correction goes further, from an error-correction algorithm for the doubled semion model to surgery on qLDPC codes: see his research. For one family of qLDPC codes there is also the bivariate bicycle code explorer.

Part 1, steps 1 to 4

Errors and decoding

  1. Step 1The repetition codeFlip qubits in a row, watch the parity checks between neighbours fire, and decode them, which on a line is a majority vote. Then simulate the logical error rate against the physical error rate for d = 1, 3, 5 and 7 and set it beside the exact curve.
  2. Step 2Lesson 1: A surface code patchStep through the encoder that spreads |0⟩ over nine qubits and eight checks, then inject an X, Y or Z error on any qubit and see which checks fire and whether the readout survives.
  3. Step 3Errors, syndromes and decodingPlace X, Z and Y errors on a d = 3, 5 or 7 patch and watch a union-find decoder grow clusters step by step. See how (d+1)/2 errors fool it, and why measurement errors need repeated rounds.
  4. Step 4The thresholdRun Monte Carlo in the browser to draw the logical against the physical error rate for d = 3, 5, 7 and up and watch the curves cross. Read off the crossing and the suppression factor, switch on measurement errors, and compare with reference numbers from stim and pymatching.

Part 2, steps 5 to 8

Computing with logical qubits

  1. Step 5Lesson 2: Logical CNOT between two patchesNine physical CNOTs between two patches act as one logical CNOT and leave a logical Bell pair. Then run the circuit live in the browser with your own noise level and an injected error.
  2. Step 6Lesson 3: Preparing a T stateMake the T state on one qubit with H and T, encode it as |T⟩​L, and compare the simulated readouts in X and Y with the ideal 14.6%.
  3. Step 7Lesson 4: Teleporting the T stateTeleport |T⟩​L from patch A to patch C through a logical Bell pair on B and C, with a Pauli fix from the measured bits.
  4. Step 8Lattice surgeryMerge two patches along an edge and split them again: the seam checks measure Z​L Z​L without reading either logical qubit. Step through it on 21 simulated qubits and run 200 shots.
