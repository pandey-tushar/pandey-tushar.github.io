Lesson 2: Logical CNOT between two patches

Both patches use the encoder from lesson 1: A becomes |+⟩​L, B becomes |0⟩​L. Nine physical CNOTs, one between each pair of matching qubits, then act as one logical CNOT and leave a logical Bell pair.

The ideal logical circuit

Run it live

Step by step

  1. prepare A. H puts q0 of A in |+⟩. A CNOT acting on |0⟩​L would do nothing; with A in |+⟩​L it makes a Bell pair.
  2. encode A. Encode A with the encoder from lesson 1. It turns q0 = |0⟩ into |0⟩​L and q0 = |1⟩ into X​L|0⟩​L = |1⟩​L, so q0 = |+⟩ becomes |+⟩​L: X​L holds, Z​L does not.
  3. encode B. Encode B the same way from q0 = |0⟩: |0⟩​L, so Z​L holds.
  4. transversal CNOT A→B. Nine CNOTs, qi of A → qi of B. Each copies X forward and Z backward: X​L of A becomes X​L of A · X​L of B, Z​L of B becomes Z​L of A · Z​L of B. That is a logical CNOT. Checks map to checks, so all sixteen hold, and one faulty CNOT hits each patch at most once.
  5. measure. Every qubit is measured in Z. Only the checks that are Z parities can still be read. The two logical bits come out random, but always equal.
