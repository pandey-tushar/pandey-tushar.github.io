In plain words

A quantum code spreads information over many qubits, and reading part of it out takes many rounds of checks. Reading several parts at once saves time.

This paper asks whether codes with a more complicated, non-commutative symmetry let one readout cover more parts than a commutative symmetry allows. It certifies 27 codes where it does: in 11 one readout covers exactly twice as many, and in 16 at least three times as many for a best choice of what to read. In simulated readouts the combined measurement used two to three times fewer rounds.

This preprint asks whether a non-commutative symmetry lets one surgery measurement read out more logical operators of a quantum code than any commutative symmetry can. This page explains the paper and lets you explore the codes it certifies.

Surgery and lifted surgery

In code surgery, the analogue of lattice surgery for quantum low-density parity-check (qLDPC) codes, a logical Pauli operator is measured by attaching an ancilla system to its support, running O(d) rounds of syndrome extraction in a merged code, and detaching; d is the code distance. Operators with pairwise disjoint supports can be measured in parallel, so m of them take O(d) rounds instead of O(md). The surface-code version of this idea can be tried interactively in the lattice surgery lesson.

Lifted surgery, from earlier work by Berent, Cohen and Quintavalle, finds such operators on codes built from Abelian (commutative) groups: a translation symmetry maps one logical operator to an orbit of them, and an orbit with pairwise disjoint supports can be measured in one merged code.

Two-block codes over non-Abelian groups

A two-block group-algebra (2BGA) code starts from a finite group G and two elements a and b of its group algebra over F2, the formal sums of group elements with coefficients 0 or 1. Its checks combine left multiplication by a with right multiplication by b on two blocks of |G| qubits. For Abelian G they include the bivariate-bicycle codes. For non-Abelian G, the left translation by g is a symmetry of the code whenever g commutes with a, and if the support of a is a union of conjugacy classes, the whole group acts this way.

Counting the gain fairly

A set of images of one Z-type logical operator under a group S of code symmetries is co-measurable if their supports are pairwise disjoint and they are linearly independent modulo the stabilisers. The co-measurable number μS is the size of the largest such set, and one merged code measures all of its operators at once. Both sides are measured against E, the group of block-affine automorphisms: qubit permutations built from the group by translating on either side, applying a group automorphism or inversion, and possibly exchanging the two blocks. The non-Abelian gain γ of a logical is μE divided by the largest μH over the Abelian subgroups H of E. A code is rigid when its only block-affine automorphisms are the translations.

Of 386 codes in which left translations beat every Abelian group of translations, 77 have twisted translations, right translations by different elements on the two blocks, and in 75 of those an Abelian subgroup of E reaches the same number.

What is proved, found and simulated

The paper classifies the block-affine automorphisms that map checks to checks and gives a criterion for rigidity. A coset-cover bound proves that the gain is at most the index of a largest Abelian subgroup of E, so an Abelian subgroup of index two caps the gain at two. A merged-distance lemma shows that the parallel gadget keeps both code distances whenever every ancilla graph has Cheeger constant at least one: every set of at most half its vertices has at least as many edges leaving it as vertices.

With E, the co-measurable numbers and the distance computed exactly, a gain of exactly two holds in ten rigid codes with distances from six to thirteen and in one non-rigid code. In four of them, [[72,8,6]], [[84,4,11]], [[108,4,13]] and [[112,4,13]], one merged code reads out every logical qubit, while every Abelian group reaches half of them. In groups built from A4, S4 and SL(2,3), where every Abelian subgroup has index at least three, 16 rigid codes reach gain at least three for a best logical, 13 of them for the code as a whole, up to [[336,26,12]]; one, [[288,20,6]], has gain 10/3. The code search, stopped after 30 of 295 queued groups, found no gain above two, and rigid gain-two codes with estimated distance up to 20, which the paper treats as evidence and not as certified.

In circuit-level simulations with Relay-BP decoding, at error rates from 2×10−3 to 5×10−3 (3 and 4×10−3 for [[240,18,10]]), the full gadget on each of the four non-Abelian codes simulated is more reliable than the Abelian gadgets it replaces or indistinguishable from them, and it uses two to three times fewer rounds.

What the paper does not claim

The paper does not compute the full permutation automorphism group, so code automorphisms of no group-theoretic origin are not excluded. The gain belongs to a chosen logical, and three of the 16 gain-three codes have gain two at code level. The measured operators are fixed by the symmetry, so the gadget helps to the extent that a computation needs them. The simulated differences are resolved only close to the pseudo-thresholds of the codes, two codes have only 1000 shots per point, the derived sequential baselines are optimistic, and all runs are Z-basis experiments.

What you could do next

The paper leaves open whether the gain grows further with the cover bound and whether gains of three persist at distances well above twelve. It names lower error rates, the fault distance of the merged protocol, X-basis experiments and a symmetry-free baseline as still needed. The repository maps each table and figure to a script and a results file, with the outputs included, so each step can be checked without rerunning it.
