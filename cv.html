Résumé · Quantum Résumé · AI

I prove theorems and ship systems. My doctoral work produced original results at the frontier of quantum topology, including a sole-authored proof of a volume conjecture. I carried that rigor into applied quantum computing and AI: an error-correction algorithm for the doubled semion model, begun at Oak Ridge; a test-time reasoning framework shipped at Agnostiq; and LLM inference infrastructure at DataRobot that serves many tenants at scale and is being hardened for government-grade security. Along the way I won the 2026 Global Industry Challenge at Quantum World Congress, a challenge at the 2025 IBM Quantum Developer Conference, the Classiq challenge at MIT iQuHACK 2024, and first prize at the Q-Volution Hackathon 2026.

Selected highlights

  • Invented Adaptive Graph of Thoughts (AGoT), a test-time reasoning framework unifying chain, tree, and graph structures; shipped at Agnostiq as a production agentic-AI blueprint, with the code open source. arXiv:2502.05078
  • Sole-authored a proof of the Bonahon–Wong–Yang volume conjecture for the four-puncture sphere; independent results at the frontier of quantum topology. arXiv:2311.13151
  • Built production multi-tenant LLM inference at scale at DataRobot: co-author of TokenGrid, a token-aware capacity scheduler; now building disaggregated LLM serving and hardening the platform for government-grade deployments. DataRobot blog
  • Cut LLM fine-tuning cost by 99% at Agnostiq with a CPU-first, federated architecture that matched quality at a fraction of the spend.
  • Gave a new explicit error detection and correction algorithm for the doubled semion model, in a review of topological phases of matter and lattice twists begun as an NSF-MSGI researcher at Oak Ridge National Laboratory; sole author, APL Quantum 2025. doi:10.1063/5.0255351
  • Won four quantum challenges and hackathons: the 2026 Global Industry Challenge at Quantum World Congress (600+ applicants from 50+ countries), the Schwinger-model hadron dynamics challenge at the IBM Quantum Developer Conference 2025, the Classiq general-arithmetic challenge at MIT iQuHACK 2024, and first prize at the Q-Volution Hackathon 2026.
