# An Unpolarized Life: chapter arc

Twelve chapters, one night. The Seeker drives to a house at the end of a road with a
question they have to answer by Friday. The book never says what the question is.

**The object.** A tin on the windowsill holds two squares of dark glass, a photographer's
filters. One held to the window dims the world and leaves it looking whole. Two turned
against each other go black, and you stop seeing the world and start seeing the glass.
Both facts are ordinary optics. Nothing in this book needs quantum mechanics to be true,
and nothing in it claims quantum mechanics is true of a person.

**The clock.** Late afternoon in chapter 1, first grey light in chapter 12. Light is the
only structure the book keeps.

**The Oracle.** A voice that has watched longer. The conceit is never explained. Four
chapters have the Oracle refuse a physics-flavoured comfort out loud: 4, 8, 9, 12.

---

## Movement I: The Turning

**1. Must I decide now?**
Human question: the pressure of a deadline on a thing that has not finished being
thought. Image: the filter forcing one orientation. A question admits two answers and
discards everything not lying that way, and what comes back looks like the whole light.
The Oracle refuses the flattering version, that an open question is always wisdom, and
gives a test: name what you learned this week.
*Status: written.*

**2. What did choosing cost me?**
Human question: grief for a life not lived. Image: what the filter discards is absorbed
and does not go anywhere you can visit. The Oracle refuses the many-worlds consolation
flatly, on the grounds of what it does to a person to have an imaginary rival. The real
cost turns out to be smaller, duller, countable.
*Status: written.*

**3. How much can I hold at once?**
Human question: five things at once and none of them moving. Image: capacity, held at
the epigraph only. This chapter deliberately runs with almost no filter talk so the image
does not become a tic. Attention alternates rather than divides. The useful cut is between
what you are working on and what you are only carrying.
*Status: written.*

## Movement II: The Cost of Looking

**4. Why does being watched change me?**
Human question: the self that exists in meetings. Image: looking as an act with a price.
**Limit named in text**: the Oracle refuses the observer-effect analogy outright. A photon
does not know it is being looked at; you do, and that difference is the whole mechanism.
What survives as a picture is narrower: a question wide enough admits more of a person,
and almost nobody asks one.
*Status: written.*

**5. What am I entitled to keep private?**
Human question: the part of you that comes out wrong in every form you fill in. Image: the
part that casts no shadow. Some of what a person is moves no count anyone takes, and that
does not make it unreal. The Oracle then names the abuse of the same claim: privacy is the
most common cover for unkindness. Test offered: does keeping it spend anyone but you.
*Status: written.*

**6. Why do I forget, and what does remembering cost?**
Human question: losing a dead father's voice. Image: the price of erasure.
**Limit named in text**: the Seeker reaches for the physical law about erasure and the
Oracle declines to use it, on the grounds that it would make the Oracle sound wiser than
it is. Holding is work you can feel, and that is all the evidence the claim needs.
*Status: written.*

**7. Can what broke be repaired?**
Human question: a friend not called in four years. Image: error correction, stripped to
redundancy. A repair is not a restoration and carries the break visibly. A thing survives
because more than one strand holds it up, and the skill is counting the strands before
they go.
*Status: written.*

## Movement III: What Comes Through

**8. What is irreplaceable about me?**
Human question: whether they will manage without you. Image: the thing that cannot be
copied. **Limit named in text**: the Oracle refuses no-cloning as a guarantee about
persons. Everything about you could be described. What cannot be repeated is the order
things happened in and the fact that you were the one they happened to.
*Status: written.*

**9. Does distance mean separation?**
Human question: a sister in Auckland and the days you know something is wrong. Image:
correlation, handled carefully. **Limit named in text, hardest**: the Oracle states that
the correlation carries no message, that this is a theorem rather than an equipment
problem, and that every book selling it as human connection kept the part that sounded
useful and threw away the part that made it true. What survives is weaker and truer: a
shared beginning showing up twice. You still have to make the call.
*Status: written.*

**10. How do I know what I know?**
Human question: the colleague who tells you your plan will fail. Image: two glasses
crossed. One filter shows you a world; two turned against each other show you the filter.
The good doubter says where and when it breaks, so you can go and check. The other kind
hands you a mood, which cannot be checked. Watch who is willing to be embarrassed.
*Status: written.*

**11. What do I owe to what I cannot see?**
Human question: four people downstream of Friday. Image: the unmeasured. The Oracle caps
the obligation at one honest pass, on the grounds that unbounded conscience is a way of
getting nothing done. Then the nearer debt: the version of you who will be holding the
result in twenty years and is not in the room for any of this week's arguments.
*Status: written.*

**12. What survives me?**
Human question: what is left. Image: conservation, and endings. **Limit named in text**:
the Oracle refuses the nothing-is-ever-destroyed comfort, on the grounds that a man who
believes he continues is careless with the part of him that does not. What survives is
effects. The Friday question is never answered.
*Status: written.*

## Considered and cut

Two questions from the spec's candidate list were folded rather than given chapters, to
hold the book under twelve and keep the pace:

- **"What is irreplaceable about me?" and "How much can I hold at once?"** both survived,
  so nothing from the spec list was dropped outright. The spec listed twelve; this is
  those twelve, reordered for an emotional arc rather than a physics one.

Candidates that were tried and abandoned during drafting, recorded so they are not
re-proposed:

- **"Why is the good version of me so expensive to run?"** Image: the cold that a fragile
  state needs. Cut because every honest draft turned into either self-help or a claim
  about human physiology, and neither is this book.
- **"Can two people be the same person?"** Image: indistinguishable particles. Cut on the
  framing rule. There is no version of it that does not read as a claim about persons.
