# Spec 04: An Unpolarized Life (working title)

## Goal

A short new book in the same two voices, about living rather than about machines.

The organizing image is a polarizing filter. A polarizer admits one orientation and discards everything else, and what comes through looks whole because you never see what was removed. Most of what a person loses in a life, they lose at the moment they force a question to hold a single answer.

The book is about how someone looks, not about what physics does inside them. Quantum ideas appear only as pictures a reader can carry: two truths held without collapsing either, what attention costs the thing attended to, whether what breaks can be repaired. No equations, no citations, no confidence-labelled predictions. Readable in one sitting. A reader who does not know what a qubit is should never feel excluded, and a physicist should never wince.

## Non-goals

- Not a simplification of The Qubit Dialogues. It is a new work that shares a voice and a cast.
- Not self-help. No steps, no promises, no bolded takeaways.
- Not a quantum-consciousness book. See the framing rule, which governs every page.

## The framing rule

The book never claims that quantum mechanics governs human experience. Not consciousness, not connection, not healing, not outcomes. The title exists partly to mark that line: this is life seen without a polarized lens, which is a claim about looking, not a claim about mechanism.

What the book does assert is narrower and true. Certain habits of holding uncertainty, the kind a physicist is trained into, are useful in a life. The polarizer is an image for a mental move. It is not a process running inside a person.

**Test every passage this way**: replace the physics word with "a filter" or "a picture". If the sentence still means what it meant, it is safe. If it loses force, the sentence was quietly relying on the physics being literally true of people. Rewrite it.

Where a chapter's metaphor runs close to that line, let the Oracle name the limit inside the text. A voice that marks the edge of its own picture reads as more trustworthy, not less.

## Shape

Twelve to fourteen short chapters, each opening on a question a person actually asks. Candidates, drawn from ideas already latent in the original and re-read through the polarizer:

| Question | Image behind it |
|---|---|
| Must I decide now? | the filter forcing one orientation |
| What did choosing cost me? | everything the filter discarded |
| Why does being watched change me? | looking as an act with a price |
| Can what broke be repaired? | error correction |
| Why do I forget, and what does remembering cost? | the price of erasure |
| What is irreplaceable about me? | the thing that cannot be copied |
| How much can I hold at once? | capacity and its limits |
| What am I entitled to keep private? | the part that casts no shadow |
| Does distance mean separation? | correlation, handled carefully |
| How do I know what I know? | the skeptic's gift |
| What do I owe to what I cannot see? | the unmeasured |
| What survives me? | conservation, and endings |

Structure per chapter: a short opening scene, the dialogue, then a reflection turned back to the reader. Reuse the `reflect` block pattern from the original. No science asides, no prediction cards.

Target 8,000 to 12,000 words. Shorter than the original by design. This book earns its place by being finishable in an evening.

## Voice

Same Seeker, same Oracle, same refusal to comfort cheaply. One adjustment: in the original the Oracle is a voice from a few decades ahead in a technical field. Here it reads better as a voice that has simply watched longer. Keep the conceit ambiguous rather than re-explaining it.

Standing prose rules carry over: no em-dashes, no balanced "not X but Y" chains, no triads, no fortune-cookie closer on every speech. Let chapters end plainly.

## Naming

Working title: **An Unpolarized Life**, from Tushar's framing.

The word carries its ordinary meaning, so the title lands for a general reader with no physics at all, while the optical sense quietly states the thesis. Variants worth weighing before committing:

- An Unpolarized Life
- Life, Unpolarized
- Without a Polarizer
- What the Filter Discards

It should not be titled as a sequel. Decision is Tushar's.

## QA setup

**Automated (blocking):**
- Shared harness from `00-overview.md`.
- **Tell census**: same fingerprint sweep as the original edition. Fresh prose drifts toward AI cadence unless measured.
- **Jargon gate**: a wordlist of technical vocabulary (qubit, decoherence, Hamiltonian, fidelity, entanglement) with a hard cap per chapter and zero tolerance inside reflections. The book's premise is that it works without them.
- **Length and pacing**: word count per chapter, flagging any chapter more than 50 percent above the median.

**Human, blocking:**
- **Framing audit** (owner: Tushar, reading as a physicist): apply the replace-the-word test to every chapter, and ask whether a motivated reader could quote any passage as a claim that quantum mechanics explains human experience. Any yes gets rewritten, not softened.
- **Read-aloud test**: it is a dialogue. Read it out. Lines that are hard to speak were written to be admired.
- **Outsider read**: one reader with no physics background. Mark where they feel lectured or lost. Where they quote a line back unprompted, that is the book working.

## Points that decide whether this is merely good

1. **The framing rule is the whole game.** Held, this is a book a working physicist can hand to anyone. Broken once, it is indistinguishable from the shelf it would otherwise sit beside.
2. **Organize by human question, not by physics topic.** The moment the chapters are secretly a quantum curriculum in disguise, readers feel the trick.
3. **The polarizer image has to actually work.** It is the spine. If a chapter cannot be told through what a filter admits and discards, that chapter probably belongs in a different book.
4. **Shortness is a feature.** Resist growth. Twelve chapters that land beat twenty-two that explain.
5. **This edition is the one most likely to reach people.** The original has a curiosity gate on it. This one has none, which raises its quality bar rather than lowering it.
